## Monday | Mistake + fix | HVAC | 194 words

Your furnace ad costs real money per click.
Then it sends people to a page with a dozen other places to go.

This month I looked at a handful of HVAC pages running Google Ads.
Most still had the full site menu on them.
About us. Careers. Financing. Blog. All one tap away.

Here's why that costs booked jobs.
Someone's furnace quit last night. The house is 58 degrees.
They clicked your ad for one thing: a tech today.
Every extra link is a chance to wander off and call someone else.

The fix takes an afternoon:
1. Build one page per service you advertise. Furnace repair ads get a furnace repair page.
2. Strip the menu. Keep your logo, phone number, and one short form.
3. Put the phone number at the top, tap-to-call on mobile.
4. Say "same-day furnace repair" and your service area in the first screen.
5. Keep thank-you pages out of the menu so your conversion tracking stays honest.

Your homepage is built for browsing.
An ad page is built for one call.

This is the first thing I check in a free Google Ads audit.

## Wednesday | Tactical tip | Plumbing | 171 words

Somebody searched "plumber jobs near me" last night.
You paid for that click.

Industry benchmarks show the average Google Ads click got pricier again in 2025.
So every wasted click hurts more than it used to.
For plumbers, the waste usually comes from the same three groups: job hunters, DIYers, and parts shoppers.

Here's the negative keyword list I'd start with:
1. jobs, careers, hiring, salary, apprentice
2. diy, how to, video, youtube
3. parts, supplies, home depot, lowes
4. school, course, license, training
5. free, cheap (test these, some areas still book fine)

Then do the thing most accounts skip.
Open the search terms report every week.
Not your keywords. The actual words people typed.

That's where you find "how to unclog a toilet with dish soap" quietly eating your budget at 11pm.

A burst pipe at 6am is the call you want.
Someone halfway through a tutorial is not.

Not sure what your search terms look like? I check them in every free Google Ads audit.

## Friday | Audit-led | Roofing | 197 words

After a hailstorm, every roofer in town turns their ads up.
The budget goes fast. Not always well.

When a roofing company asks me for a free Google Ads audit, I don't start with the fancy stuff.
I check three things first.

1. Where the calls go.
Is call tracking set up? Are calls counted as conversions? Or is Google chasing form fills from people who wanted a gutter quote?

2. Who sees the ads.
Location targeting left on "presence or interest" can show your ads to people who don't live in your area.
That's how a roofer in Dallas pays for clicks from Ohio.

3. What happens after the click.
Does the page say roof repair, insurance claims, and your city in the first screen?
Is there a phone number I can tap?

Those three explain most of the wasted spend I see.
